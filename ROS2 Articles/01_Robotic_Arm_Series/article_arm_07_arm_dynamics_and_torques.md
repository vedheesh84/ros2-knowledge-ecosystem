## ARM 07: ARM DYNAMICS, TORQUES & GRAVITY COMPENSATION

*Purpose: Bridge geometry with physics. Formulate the dynamic equations of motion of a robotic manipulator using the Euler-Lagrange equations, understand inertia matrices, Coriolis/centrifugal forces, gravity loading, motor torque limits, and feedforward gravity compensation.*

### Must Answer
- What is the difference between Kinematics (motion without forces) and Dynamics (forces causing motion)?
- What are the Euler-Lagrange equations of motion, and what physical phenomena do $M(\mathbf{q}), C(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{\dot{q}}), \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q})$ represent?
- Why do joint torques vary non-linearly with robot posture, and which joint experiences the highest gravitational load?
- What is Feedforward Gravity Compensation, and how does it prevent the robot arm from sagging under payload?
- How do motor torque limits, gear reductions, and back-EMF establish physical velocity and payload ceilings?

### Key Insight
A robot arm holding a payload at full horizontal extension requires maximum motor torque just to remain stationary against gravity; without dynamic modeling, simple feedback controllers will either sag or oscillate aggressively.

### 1. The Equations of Motion: Euler-Lagrange Formulation

The dynamic behavior of an $n$-DOF articulated robotic manipulator is described by the matrix differential equation:

$$M(\mathbf{q}) \mathbf{\ddot{q}} + C(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{\dot{q}}) \mathbf{\dot{q}} + \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) + \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{friction}} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{actuator}}$$

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      DYNAMIC EQUATION DECONSTRUCTION                        │
│                                                                             │
│   M(q) \ddot{q}  ──▶ Mass & Inertia Matrix (Resistance to acceleration)     │
│   C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} ──▶ Coriolis & Centrifugal Matrix (Velocity-coupling)   │
│   g(q)           ──▶ Gravity Vector (Torque needed to oppose gravity)       │
│   \tau_friction  ──▶ Viscous and Coulomb friction in gearboxes & bearings   │
│   \tau_actuator  ──▶ Commanded motor torques at joint axes                   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### 1. The Mass/Inertia Matrix $M(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$:
- Symmetric ($M = M^T$) and Positive-Definite ($\mathbf{v}^T M \mathbf{v} > 0, \forall \mathbf{v} \ne 0$).
- $M_{ii}$ is the effective inertia of joint $i$; off-diagonal terms $M_{ij}$ represent dynamic cross-coupling (accelerating Joint 2 induces a reaction torque on Joint 1).

#### 2. The Coriolis & Centrifugal Matrix $C(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{\dot{q}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$:
- **Centrifugal terms** ($\dot{q}_i^2$): Outward forces generated by high-speed rotational motion.
- **Coriolis terms** ($\dot{q}_i \dot{q}_j$): Coupling forces when two connected links rotate simultaneously.

#### 3. The Gravity Vector $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^n$:
- Represents the static torque generated by the mass of all distal links and the grasped payload acting on joint $i$:
  $$g_i(\mathbf{q}) = \frac{\partial U(\mathbf{q})}{\partial q_i}$$
  where $U(\mathbf{q})$ is the total gravitational potential energy of the arm.

### 2. Static Gravity Loading on the 5-DOF Articulated Arm

When the robot is holding a stationary position ($\mathbf{\dot{q}} = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{\ddot{q}} = \mathbf{0}$), the dynamic equation collapses to:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{static}} = \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q})$$

For Shoulder Joint 2 and Elbow Joint 3 holding masses $m_2, m_3, m_{\text{payload}}$:
$$\tau_{\text{shoulder}} = m_2 g r_{c2} \cos(q_2) + m_3 g [a_2 \cos(q_2) + r_{c3} \cos(q_2+q_3)] + m_{\text{payload}} g [a_2 \cos(q_2) + a_3 \cos(q_2+q_3) + d_5 \cos(q_2+q_3+q_4)]$$

#### Critical Physical Insight:
- **Maximum Shoulder Torque**: Occurs at $q_2 = 0, q_3 = 0, q_4 = 0$ (Horizontal reach, lever arm is maximized).
- **Minimum Shoulder Torque**: Occurs at $q_2 = +90^\circ$ (Arm pointing straight up, lever arm is zero).

### 3. Feedforward Gravity Compensation

In standard PD joint control ($\tau = K_p (q_{\text{des}} - q) - K_d \dot{q}$), a steady-state positioning error (sag) is inevitable unless $K_p \to \infty$ (which causes instability). 

By adding a feedforward gravity model:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{command}} = \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{q}) + K_p (\mathbf{q}_{\text{des}} - \mathbf{q}) + K_d (\mathbf{\dot{q}}_{\text{des}} - \mathbf{\dot{q}})$$

The motor applies the exact holding torque required by physics, allowing the PD controller to handle only small dynamic disturbances.
